The strongest aspect of my project is the way the customer problem is treated as genuinely multivariate rather than as three separate RFM scorecards. I first model Recency, Frequency and Monetary value as a joint customer vector, examine its covariance structure, transform the highly skewed variables, and then use PCA before clustering. The workflow also links exploratory segmentation to formal inference through MANOVA and Hotelling's T-squared, and finally to prediction using Linear Discriminant Analysis. A second strength is the prospective churn definition. I compute predictors only from the historical period and classify churn according to whether the customer purchases in the following 91-day window. This reduces the direct target leakage that would occur if churn were simply defined as high current Recency.

A limitation is that LDA assumes approximately multivariate normal class distributions and a common covariance matrix. In this dataset, normality tests remain significant even after log transformation and winsorization, and Box's M test rejects covariance equality. Therefore, LDA is useful as an interpretable course-aligned baseline, but its assumptions are not perfectly satisfied. An alternative I considered is Quadratic Discriminant Analysis, which permits class-specific covariance matrices. Logistic regression or tree-based classifiers could also improve predictive flexibility, but QDA is the most direct multivariate alternative to LDA within this problem.
